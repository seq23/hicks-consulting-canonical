What to Expect at Your First Session With a Black Therapist

Your first session with a Black therapist is an initial assessment where you discuss your history, current stressors, and goals. It is a structured conversation to determine if the therapist's style meets your needs. This meeting establishes the professional relationship and outlines how future sessions will address burnout, anxiety, or life transitions.

Published 3 November 2026 · By Monika Hicks, LCSW

How does the first session begin?

The first meeting often starts with a review of administrative details. You will likely discuss privacy policies and the professional boundaries of the relationship. Reading this information is not therapy. If you are in a crisis, please call or text 988 immediately. The therapist will explain how virtual individual therapy for adults in Tennessee works. They will also clarify the difference between clinical therapy and virtual coaching. Coaching is non-clinical and focuses on specific life goals rather than mental health diagnoses. You may also discuss the option for a Christian, faith-integrated approach. This is only included if you prefer it and is never imposed on any client.

The environment is designed to be a professional space for your concerns. You do not need to have everything figured out before you arrive. The therapist will guide the conversation to help you feel grounded. This is a time to talk about the stress or relationship strain you are carrying. You can ask questions about the process at any time. The goal is to ensure you understand the framework of the support provided. This session sets the tone for your future work together.

What questions will the therapist ask?

You can expect questions about your current life situation and what brought you to seek support now. The therapist may ask about your family history, physical health, and social support systems. They want to understand the context of your life as a Black woman in Tennessee. This includes discussing work-life balance and any burnout you may be feeling. They might ask about your sleep patterns or how anxiety affects your daily routine. These questions help the therapist gather a complete picture of your well-being.

The conversation may also cover your past experiences with mental health care. If you are interested in organizational mental health training for your workplace, you can mention that interest. The therapist will listen to your narrative without judgment. They are looking to understand your strengths as well as your challenges. You are encouraged to be as open as you feel comfortable being. There is no requirement to share everything in the first hour. Building trust is a gradual process that begins with these initial questions.

  • Your reasons for seeking therapy or coaching at this time.
  • Details about your support system and family relationships.
  • Current symptoms of stress, anxiety, or burnout.
  • Your personal goals for the therapeutic process.
  • Any preferences regarding faith-integrated care.

Can I ask the therapist questions?

Yes, the first session is a two-way street. You are encouraged to ask about the therapist's approach to care. You might ask how they handle life transitions or relationship strain. It is helpful to know how they incorporate cultural competence into their practice. You can inquire about their experience working with Black women specifically. Understanding their methods helps you decide if the fit feels right for you. A grounded and plain explanation of their process can ease your mind.

You may also ask about the logistics of the practice. While fees and insurance are not discussed here, you can ask about the frequency of sessions. You might want to know more about support groups or consulting services offered. If you are looking for a Black Therapist in Memphis, you can confirm their service area within Tennessee. Asking questions helps you take an active role in your mental health journey. It ensures that the professional relationship is built on clear communication and mutual understanding.

What happens after the session ends?

At the end of the meeting, you and the therapist will discuss the next steps. You might decide to schedule a follow-up appointment if the fit feels appropriate. The therapist may provide some initial reflections on what you shared. They might suggest whether individual therapy or a support group would be more beneficial for your situation. There is no obligation to continue if you do not feel the connection is right. Finding the right professional is an important part of the process.

You may leave the session with a sense of what to expect moving forward. Some people feel a sense of relief after speaking openly for the first time. Others may feel tired as discussing personal history can be draining. Both reactions are common and valid. You can take time to process the conversation before making your next move. If you decide to proceed, you will work together to create a plan that addresses your specific needs. This plan will guide your future sessions and help you navigate life's challenges.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the first session usually last?

Length varies by practice, so ask when you book. At Hicks Consulting the first full appointment is the intake, and ongoing sessions run 60 or 30 minutes. Knowing the length ahead of time lets you protect enough private time to talk without rushing through your history or your current concerns.

Is my first session confidential?

Confidentiality is a fundamental part of the therapeutic relationship. Your therapist will explain the legal and ethical limits of privacy during the first meeting. Generally, what you discuss remains private unless there is a risk of harm to yourself or others, as required by Tennessee state law.

What if I feel nervous about the first meeting?

It is very common to feel nervous before your first session. Many people find that the anxiety decreases once the conversation begins. A Black therapist understands the unique pressures you face and aims to provide a warm, grounded space where you can feel heard and respected.

Do I need to prepare anything beforehand?

You do not need to prepare a formal presentation. It can be helpful to think about your main concerns and what you hope to achieve. Having a quiet, private space for your virtual session is important. This helps you focus on the conversation without outside distractions or interruptions.
